# Sources for deck descriptions and histories

Checked online on **2026-09-26**. The interface uses short paraphrases, with links
beside each deck's story. Content is bundled with the app; opening a gallery or
Settings does not fetch third-party pages. The source links in the Cards gallery
open only when chosen.

## Modiano

- [Spielkarten-Wiki: Industrie und Glück (Q909)](https://spielkarten.wiki/wiki/Q909)
  lists *Tarok Študentski servis Maribor* under this pattern. Its pattern record
  identifies French suits, double-ended imagery, 54 cards, and the inscription
  on trump II. These support the description, without inferring a particular
  pattern subtype or an individual artist.
- [Wikimedia Commons: photographed Pagat from the Maribor pack](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SPKW-Q2171-IMG_8529-tarock-1.jpg)
  identifies the pack as *Tarok Študentski servis Maribor*, dates it to 1995,
  credits S. Modiano and names photographer Martin Okrslar. The interface
  attributes this date to the photographed copy's catalogue record; it does
  not present 1995 as the origin of the historical pattern.
- [Modiano: Our history](https://www.modiano.it/en/our-history/)
  places the company's founding in Trieste in 1868. This is company history,
  not a date for the Maribor edition or its illustrations.

## Slovenski tarok · Piatnik

- [Slovenski tarok: presentation and gallery](https://slovenski-tarok.si/)
  explains the deck's Slovenian cultural, historical, and narrative subjects.
  Its card captions identify skiers from Bloke, construction of a double
  hayrack, and Urška with the Water Man.
- [Slovenski tarok: history](https://slovenski-tarok.si/zgodovina)
  credits Janez Bogataj's concept, Matjaž Schmidt's illustrations, commissioner
  Eurotrade Commerce of Ljubljana, and printer Piatnik of Vienna.
  **1995 is when Schmidt began the illustrations**, not a confirmed publication
  year. The UI preserves that distinction in every language.
- The [Slovenian Ethnographic Museum's publication announcement](https://www.etno-muzej.si/sl/node/47616)
  confirms his occupation as an ethnologist.

## Smrekarjev tarok · Hinko Smrekar

- [National Gallery of Slovenia: Slovanski tarok](https://smrekar.ng-slo.si/slovanski-tarok/)
  dates the catalogued works to **1910–1912** and identifies the commissioner as
  Prva slovanska tovarna igralnih kart in Ljubljana. It records the Ljubljana
  police prohibition on further distribution in **1916**. The artwork date is
  not stated as a publication year.
- The same museum page describes Slavic customs and traditional dress, including
  Kurent ploughing (III), the Moreška sword dance from Korčula (VI), and Gail
  Valley barrel striking (XII). The UI includes these documented motifs and
  omits identifications which the museum itself treats as uncertain.

Translations preserve the same facts. Source credits and the app's reconstructed
or restored card faces are separate from these histories; see [cards.md](cards.md).
